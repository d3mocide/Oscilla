Dual-Radio Breadboard Layout

A hole-by-hole assembly guide for a horizontal XIAO ESP32-C5 and Wio-SX1262, plus the off-board CC1101. It shows which header row faces the power rails, every C5↔Wio jumper, where the CC1101 branches onto the shared SPI bus, and exactly where the pull resistors and local capacitor pairs belong.

First: how power should run

One source

The XIAO 3V3 pin is the only source for both radio modules. Feed the upper + rail once from XIAO 3V3 and the upper − rail once from XIAO GND. Do not feed either rail from a second regulator or USB source.

Two rail locations

The upper rails power the modules and their capacitor pairs. Bridge upper + to lower + and upper − to lower − once so the lower rails can terminate the three Wio pull resistors. That is distribution, not a second feed.

Prove every segment

Many breadboards split a rail at the centre. With power off, continuity-map the exact board in front of you and bridge each split you intend to use. A printed red/blue line does not prove the metal underneath is continuous.

Physical orientation used throughout this guide: both XIAO-format boards are horizontal, straddle the centre trench, and put their power/SPI header row toward the upper rails. On the XIAO, the USB connector is at the right. Read the Wio's signal-name silkscreen, not its passthrough D-number label.
Why breadboard this at all. Rev E's own status line reads "Proposed wiring; electrical and RF bench validation pending," and D-15 is still a leaning rather than a decision. You are testing a proposal, so a build you can take apart matches what this actually is. It also makes the passives trivial — a pull-up stops being "solder a resistor onto a wire mid-air" and becomes "plug a resistor between this strip and the power rail," which is the job breadboard rails exist for.

What a breadboard can and can't prove

Rev E §6 lists seven gates before this wiring counts as working. A breadboard is perfectly good evidence for some of them and actively misleading for others, so it's worth knowing which is which before you start drawing conclusions from it.

GateOn a breadboardWhy
1 · ContinuityTrust itDC continuity. If anything it's easier to probe than a soldered harness.
2 · Boot state (CS high through reset)Trust itA DC logic-level question. The pull-ups behave identically.
3 · SPI status responseValid for this buildA deterministic response at 1 MHz proves the breadboard path works. It does not establish the final harness's speed or margin.
4 · MISO isolationValid for this buildPreserving CC1101 IOCFG1=0x2E, correct Wio reads, and a clean trace are useful breadboard evidence. Repeat on the final harness.
5 · ReceiveIndicative only"It decodes a known test vector" is meaningful when frequency, modulation, deviation, rate, bandwidth, packet settings, source, distance, and antenna are recorded. Absolute RSSI/noise-floor results are not transferable.
6 · CoexistenceDon't trust itThis is exactly the measurement breadboard parasitics and long unshielded jumpers corrupt. False events and loss rate here tell you about the breadboard.
7 · Power at module pinsDon't trust itContact resistance and rail drop are the breadboard's, not the final build's. D-4 / P6 needs the real harness.
So: exercise gates 1–4 on the breadboard, treat gate 5 as a smoke test, and repeat every accepted gate on the soldered harness before phase exit. Record breadboard results as breadboard results — this directory's own rule is that a number without its conditions can't set a threshold, and "measured on a breadboard" is a condition.

The footprint trap

The Wio has the same footprint as the XIAO. Do not wire it straight across, pin for pin. It is a stacking shield, so the tidy-looking thing — a parallel bundle of jumpers from each XIAO pin to the matching Wio pin — is exactly the stacked pinout Rev D §4 forbids twice: "Do not assume a stacked shield pinout," and "This differs from direct stacking."

Two specific things break if you wire position-to-position:

  • XIAO D0 does not go to the Wio pad marked D0. It goes to the pad marked RST. Rev D §2 calls this out by name as its worked example, and the Wio's own D0 pad is on the list of pads to leave unconnected.
  • Wio VIN, D0, D6 and D7 must stay unconnected (Rev D §4.1). D6/D7 are the host UART on the XIAO side — a straight-across bundle would drag the Grove link into the radio harness.

The root cause is that the Wio silkscreens two labels at every pin: a passthrough XIAO position label and the module's real signal name. AGENTS.md gotcha 16 exists because the wrong set was nearly used once already, and lora-harness.md records the near-miss. On a breadboard, with two identical footprints sitting side by side and you counting columns, that mistake is easier to make, not harder.

Rule for this build: wire by signal name, never by position. Every row of the wiring table names the Wio pad by its signal silkscreen (RST, NSS, BUSY, DIO1, RF_SW…). If you find yourself reading a D-number on the Wio, stop and find the signal name printed at the same pin.

Board layout — horizontal, with the real header order

The drawing below uses a concrete example: XIAO on breadboard columns 8–14 and Wio on 22–28. Different column numbers are fine; preserve the orientation, leave at least seven empty columns between the boards, and copy the signal names. Each vertical five-hole strip is one net; strips above and below the centre trench are separate even when they share a printed column number.

shared SPIWio control 3V3ground leave open
Do not copy the printed column number across the centre trench. For example, “column 12 above” and “column 12 below” are isolated. The XIAO and Wio straddle that trench specifically so their two header rows do not short together.

Build the shared SPI bus deliberately

A five-hole strip is one electrical node, but the XIAO and Wio are on different strips. The bus is therefore three explicit XIAO→Wio jumpers, with the CC1101 branching from spare holes on the three XIAO strips. Do the same thing for SCK, MISO and MOSI; do not daisy-chain through a radio module's header.

Never join the chip selects. Wio NSS remains on XIAO D4 / GPIO23; CC1101 CSn remains on XIAO D3 / GPIO7. Firmware selects one receiver by pulling only its own CS low. If both are low, both radios can drive MISO and corrupt or electrically contend on the bus.

Wire it in five passes

  1. Seat and power the XIAO only. Place it horizontal with USB at right. Join split rail segments, feed upper + from XIAO 3V3 and upper − from XIAO GND, then bridge those rails once to the lower +/− pair. Power off; confirm + to − is not shorted before continuing.
  2. Add Wio power and its local capacitors. Seat the Wio horizontal with its 3V3/GND/SPI row facing upward. Run Wio 3V3 to upper + and Wio GND to upper −. Put 100 nF and 10 µF in parallel between the Wio 3V3 and GND five-hole strips, immediately beside the header. 100 nF has no polarity. If 10 µF is electrolytic: + to 3V3, striped − leg to GND.
  3. Wire the complete Wio, not just SPI. Add D8→SCK, D9→MISO, D10→MOSI; then D4→NSS, D0→RST, D1→DIO1, D5→BUSY, and D2→RF_SW. Leave Wio VIN, D0, D6 and D7 positions open. BUSY is required.
  4. Add the three Wio startup pulls. From the Wio NSS and RST strips, connect separate 10 kΩ resistors to the lower + rail. From Wio RF_SW, connect a 10 kΩ resistor to the lower − rail. RF_SW is the only pull-down. A resistor is a branch; it never replaces the signal jumper.
  5. Add the CC1101 as a local six-wire pod. Branch SCK, MISO and MOSI from spare holes on XIAO D8, D9 and D10. Add XIAO D3→CC CSn, + rail→VCC, and − rail→GND. At the CC module end fit CSn→VCC 10 kΩ and the 100 nF + 10 µF pair across VCC/GND. Leave GDO0 and GDO2 open. Keep all six jumpers under about 10 cm and run GND in the same bundle.

Components and where they plug in

Eight passives total: four 10 kΩ resistors and two capacitor pairs. The build guide explains what each one does and why; this is where they physically go on the board.

A pull resistor is a third branch on a signal; it is not inline. The signal jumper remains XIAO pin ↔ radio pin. A separate resistor leg shares that signal strip and its other leg goes to the named rail. If the resistor cannot reach a rail without tension, jumper the signal to an unused nearby strip and place the resistor there. The full parts list is in the build guide.

Decoupling is the one thing a breadboard makes worse

The 100 nF's entire job is being physically close to the module's own supply pins. On the Wio, both capacitor pairs live in the spare holes of its adjacent 3V3 and GND strips. The CC1101 is off-board, so its pair must live at the module pod across VCC/GND—not back on the breadboard at the far end of the jumpers.

CC1101 — keep it off the board

The E07-M1101D-SMA is a 2×4 header, so it won't straddle the channel the way the two XIAO-footprint boards do. You have two options, and the simpler one is also better for RF:

  • Six female-to-male jumpers straight onto its header pins, module sitting beside the board. No adapter needed. Only six of its eight pins are connected in this build, so there's not much to wrangle — and it keeps the SMA and antenna away from the dense wiring, which is what Rev E §4.3 asks for anyway.
  • A 2×4 adapter or breakout if you'd rather have it captive on the board. Worth checking the header first: if it's 2.54 mm pitch with 2.54 mm between rows, the two rows land in two adjacent strips (which are independent, so that works) — the awkwardness is mechanical overhang, not electrical.
What “local pod” means. Put a tiny 2×4 adapter, a two-row female header on perfboard, or soldered short component leads immediately beside the CC1101 header. Terminate the six breadboard jumpers there. Fit the 100 nF and 10 µF directly across CC pins 2 (VCC) and 1 (GND), and the 10 kΩ directly from pin 4 (CSn) to pin 2 (VCC). If those parts remain back on the main breadboard while the CC1101 is at the end of 10 cm wires, they are not local decoupling.
Watch the jumper length. Rev D §4.3 and Rev E §4.3 both want the SPI harness under about 10 cm with a ground return alongside. Standard dupont jumpers are often 20 cm. Use the shortest you have, run a ground jumper in the same bundle rather than grounding the module somewhere else, and keep the clock at 1 MHz.

Breadboard gotchas

  • Every rail segment starts separate until you prove otherwise. Upper and lower rail pairs are not connected to each other, and many full-size boards also break each rail in the middle. Bridge upper + to lower +, upper − to lower −, bridge any centre breaks, then confirm continuity across every intended same-polarity segment.
  • Contact reliability is the failure mode here. A marginal contact gives you an intermittent fault that looks exactly like the design problems gates 3 and 4 exist to find. If something works and then doesn't, reseat before you debug. Don't use a tired board for this.
  • Reseat after moving the board. Two modules, an SMA connector and a jumper bundle put real mechanical stress on the contacts.
  • Cardputer and XIAO keep their own USB power. The Wio and CC1101 take only XIAO 3V3. Grove red stays disconnected and insulated — the breadboard doesn't change Rev D §9's prototype power rule, and P6 still hasn't happened.
  • Power sequencing still applies. Rev D §9: both endpoints powered before active signal lines are connected. On a breadboard it's tempting to plug a jumper into a live board. Don't.
  • Keep the antennas off the board and apart. Separate antennas, no splitter, feeds away from the wiring (Rev E §4.3). Two radio modules and their antennas in among a jumper bundle is not a clean RF environment, which is part of why gate 6 doesn't count here.
