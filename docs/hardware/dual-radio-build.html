Dual-Radio Backpack Build

How to physically wire the XIAO ESP32-C5 to both sub-GHz radios — what every resistor and capacitor is for, where its legs actually go, and how two radios share one SPI bus without fighting. Every pin number and component value here is copied from Rev D §4 and Rev E §2–§4. Where I explain general soldering technique rather than quoting those documents, it says so.

Where this build actually stands. The Wio-SX1262 harness is wired and proven — full reset → TCXO → calibrate → RX ran fault-free first try, and it has decoded real MeshCore traffic through a 66.8-minute soak (lora-harness.md). But it was wired without the three pull resistors Rev D §4.2 calls for: all three measured open on the delivered Wio board, and the build went ahead anyway as a deliberate bench-only call. Rev D's own standard says fit them before this leaves the bench. The CC1101 side is entirely ahead of you — it is a 🔵 leaning decision (D-15), not a built thing, and no CC1101 driver exists in firmware-c5/ yet.

Ground rules before the iron is hot

3.3 V only, on every signal and both radio supplies. The CC1101 module (E07-M1101D-SMA) is a 1.8–3.6 V part and takes its power from the XIAO's 3V3 output — Rev E §4 says explicitly: do not use a 5 V module supply. A connector that carries 5 V somewhere else does not make its signal pins 5 V tolerant (Rev D §2).
  • Both boards on their own USB. Grove red 5 V conductor disconnected and insulated, XIAO 5V/BAT pads unused. This is Rev D §9's prototype arrangement and Rev E §6 keeps it unchanged for CC1101 bring-up — a shared power arrangement is not approved on calculation alone, only on P6 measurements that have not been taken yet.
  • Power both endpoints before connecting active signal lines (Rev D §9). Pull signal wires before powering only one side. Series resistors are not isolation.
  • Keep the harness short — under about 10 cm, with a ground wire running alongside the SPI wires, not a ground that takes some other route (Rev E §4.3).
  • Inspect each module before adding parts. Both Rev D §4.2 and Rev E §3/§4 say the same thing: some of these parts may already be fitted on the breakout. Measure first, then fit what is actually missing — do not blindly duplicate, and do not place a conflicting value in parallel with one that is already there.
  • Nothing here transmits. The "never key up without an antenna" rule you may have heard is a transmit concern; this build has no reachable TX path on either radio (D-8). Fit the antennas because you want to hear anything, not because the radio will damage itself.

What you're building

One SPI controller (the C5) and two SPI peripherals that never talk at the same time. Three wires are common to both radios. Everything else is per-radio, and the chip-select line is what decides which radio is listening to the bus at any moment.

The Wio needs four extra control wires beyond SPI — reset, an interrupt line, a BUSY handshake, and an RF-switch control. The CC1101 needs none of those in this build: its reset is an SPI strobe, and its GDO pins stay unconnected while the firmware polls it (Rev E §2, §4).

Passives, explained

This is the part the wiring docs state as a one-line spec and never explain, so here is the whole idea. There are only two shapes of component placement in this build, and telling them apart is most of the battle.

Parallel — taps the wire

Pull-up

The signal still runs MCU → module exactly as normal. The resistor is an extra branch off that same wire going up to 3V3. Nothing is cut.

Parallel — taps the wire

Pull-down

Identical placement, other destination. In this whole build exactly one resistor goes to ground: the Wio's RF_SW. Getting that one backwards is the easy mistake.

Bridge — across the supply

Decoupling cap

Straight across the module's own VCC and GND pins, as physically close to them as you can get. Not in any signal path. Distance is the whole point, so short legs matter.

Series — sits in the path

Series resistor (contrast)

This one does cut the wire. Nothing in the radio harness is wired this way — it is Rev D §3's optional UART protection on a different cable, shown here only so the two shapes never blur together.

Practical notes — general technique, not from the wiring docs. Resistors and ceramic capacitors have no polarity, so orientation doesn't matter. A 10 µF electrolytic does: the stripe marks the negative leg, which goes to ground — backwards, it will eventually fail, sometimes loudly. A 10 µF ceramic sidesteps that entirely and Rev E lists either as acceptable. Keep the decoupling caps' legs short and put them at the module end of the supply wire, never at the XIAO end: a bypass cap 8 cm away from the pin it's bypassing is decoration. The pull resistors are not distance-sensitive in the same way — anywhere along the line is electrically fine, so put them wherever they're mechanically sturdy.
Measure before you fit. On the delivered Wio board, all three of its pull resistors read open, not ~10 kΩ — that's recorded in lora-harness.md, and it's why they're listed as still needed below. Do the same check on your CC1101 breakout before adding its pull-up and caps: probe CSn to VCC and VCC to GND with the module unpowered and disconnected. Roughly 10 kΩ means it's already fitted — adding a second one in parallel gives you 5 kΩ, which is a different circuit than the one specified.

The shared SPI bus

SPI is a controller-plus-peripherals bus. Three wires are genuinely common to every peripheral on it, and one more wire per peripheral picks which one is listening. That's the entire trick:

  • SCK (GPIO8) — the clock. The C5 generates it; both radios watch it.
  • MOSI (GPIO10) — controller out. The C5 talks; both radios hear it.
  • MISO (GPIO9) — controller in. Both radios can talk on this one, which is the wire that needs a rule.
  • CS, one per radio — Wio NSS on GPIO23, CC1101 CSn on GPIO7. Active low: pull one low and that radio joins the conversation.

The one rule that makes it work

SCK and MOSI are safe to share because only the C5 ever drives them. MISO is different: it's an output from each radio into one shared wire. If both radios drove it at once, one would be pushing high while the other pulls low — two outputs fighting over the same copper.

What prevents that is the deselected peripheral releasing MISO into high-impedance. The CC1101 needs an explicit configuration invariant for this: its SO pin is also GDO1, and IOCFG1.GDO1_CFG must remain at 0x2E (three-state). Any other GDO1 function may drive the shared line while CSn is high. Rev E §5.2 therefore makes both that register setting and a bench isolation check part of the shared-bus contract.

Why the CC1101 gets singled out. On the E07-M1101D-SMA, pin 7 is MISO/GDO1. TI specifies three-state as the reset default precisely so the SPI bus can be shared. Constant 0x00 or 0xFF reads are a useful symptom, but not proof: wiring, selection, timing, or the Wio can produce the same result. Confirm CS levels, preserve 0x2E, and inspect MISO with a logic analyzer or scope before assigning blame.

Arbitration — electrical vs. policy

Two separate things share the name "one at a time" here, and it's worth keeping them apart:

  • Bus lock (electrical). One SPI transaction at a time, so two tasks can't interleave bytes into the same shift register. Rev E §5: no interrupt handler performs SPI work.
  • Radio arbiter (policy). Only one sub-GHz receive engine runs at a time — not because the bus can't handle two peripherals, but because the two radios sit centimetres apart and would desensitise each other. Switching to one radio explicitly stops and deselects the other (Rev E §1, §5).

What the firmware already does

firmware-c5/main/lora_radio.c already owns this bus. It calls spi_bus_initialize() once on SPI2_HOST with sclk=8, mosi=10, miso=9, mode 0, 1 MHz, and notably sets spics_io_num = -1 — CS is driven by hand in software, because BUSY has to be checked before each select.

So adding the CC1101 on the firmware side is a second spi_bus_add_device() on the same host, not a second spi_bus_initialize(). ESP-IDF is happy with multiple devices on one host; initialising the same pins twice is the thing that fails. That driver doesn't exist yet — after you finish soldering, the Wio half is immediately testable with current firmware and the CC1101 half has nothing to run against it beyond a bring-up probe you'd have to write.

Start slow. 1 MHz, leads under 10 cm, ground alongside (Rev D §4.3, Rev E §4.3). Raise the clock only after clean transfers are measured, and expect a two-radio harness to be fussier about lead length than the single-radio one you already have working.

Resolved constraint and remaining cautions

GPIO7 strap behavior is resolved from the ESP32-C5 datasheet. GPIO7 controls the early-boot JTAG signal source; it is not a boot-mode strap and has no internal pull. With the default JTAG eFuses its sampled level is a don't-care and USB Serial/JTAG remains selected. If JTAG source selection is enabled and neither JTAG interface is disabled, high selects USB Serial/JTAG and low selects the pad-JTAG pins. The CC1101's 10 kΩ CSn pull-up therefore defines the required deselected state without changing the default boot mode. Record the delivered board's eFuse state and still run cold-boot, native-USB, and JTAG recovery tests with the pull-up fitted.
1. Rev D and Rev E disagree about GPIO7, and Rev E wins. Rev D §4.1 says "Leave XIAO D3 / GPIO7 unconnected" and its YAML carries unused_main_header_gpio: 7. Rev E §2 reallocates it as CC1101 CSn and declares itself the winner for the dual-radio changes it enumerates. If you read Rev D alone you would leave this pin bare. The YAML block in Rev D §8 has not been updated to match.
2. The CC1101 extension is a leaning, not a decision. D-15 is 🔵 Leaning, gated on P3 bench work. Rev E's own status line says "Proposed wiring; electrical and RF bench validation pending." Nothing here has been built or measured — treat the CC1101 half of this guide as a plan you're testing, not a recipe that's known to work.
3. The power budget is still unmeasured. Rev D §9's current table is explicitly provisional (C5 + Wio ≈ 910 mA with margin against a nominally 1 A rail), and the CC1101 adds to it. D-4 stays open until P6 produces real numbers. Keep both boards on separate USB and Grove red insulated until then.
